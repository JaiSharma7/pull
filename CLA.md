## Why this exists

The code in this repository is licensed to the public under the
[GNU Affero General Public License v3](./LICENSE). That is the outbound licence, and it
is not changing.

The AGPL is a strong copyleft licence, and it binds _the project_ as much as anyone
else. If a contribution arrived under the AGPL alone, the project could only ever use
it under the AGPL — which would make it impossible to offer a paid, non-core service
alongside the free one without either open-sourcing that service in full or rewriting
every contributed line it touched.

This agreement fixes that in the ordinary way: **you keep your copyright**, and you also
grant the project a licence broad enough to relicense your contribution. Nothing here
takes ownership away from you, and nothing here removes your own right to use what you
wrote however else you like.

Two consequences worth being blunt about, because you should know them before signing:

- The project **may** use your contribution in a commercial or closed-source offering.
- The project **may** release your contribution under a licence other than the AGPL.

If either is unacceptable to you, do not sign this, and do not contribute code. That is
a completely reasonable position and no one will think less of you for it.

## What this does not cover

- **Content is not code.** Summaries, submitted works, corpus entries, translations and
  anything else that is a _Pull_ rather than a _program_ are governed by a separate
  agreement, not this one. See `docs/content-policy.md` and law 4 in `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Your other work.** This agreement reaches only what you deliberately submit to this
  repository.

## How to sign

Add your name to `CONTRIBUTORS.md` in the same pull request as your first contribution,
and keep the `Signed-off-by:` line on every commit (see `CONTRIBUTING.md` — the DCO
records provenance per commit; this agreement records the licence grant once).

That line is your signature, and it means: I have read this document and I agree to it
for my present and future contributions to this repository.

No CI job enforces this — a reviewer checks it before merging a first contribution. It is
deliberately a human step: a licence grant nobody read is not worth having, and a bot
ticking it off would make it exactly that.
