# Fable 5.1 — graph engineering, and the review that motivates it

Status: **approved 2026-09-02**, full scope. Nothing here is built yet. The BAML toolchain it depends on is, and is
described in `docs/baml.md`.

Three things, in dependency order: a review of the generation pipeline as it stands, a
replacement of its step-machine with a declared graph, and the features that only become
affordable once the graph exists.

---

## 1. Review of the current state

The read path, the RLS posture and the queue mechanics are in good shape and are not the
subject of this plan. Every finding below is in the generation pipeline, which is the part
Fable 5.1 changes. Findings are ranked by what they cost, not by how easy they are to fix.

### 1.1 Every step re-reads every earlier step's output, including the source text

`worker/index.ts:144` calls `job_step_outputs(p_job_id)`, which is
`jsonb_object_agg` over **all** succeeded steps — there is no way to ask for a subset.
`acquire` stores up to `MAX_SOURCE_CHARS` (200,000) characters of source text at
`pipeline.ts:611`, and `chunk` then stores the _same text again_, split into sections, at
`pipeline.ts:628`.

So from step 4 onward every invocation pulls roughly 400 kB of JSON out of Postgres to
run a step that, in most cases, reads one small field of it. `moderate` reads
`job.target` and nothing else; `artwork` reads nothing at all. Eight of the twelve steps
pay for the whole blob.

|                                   |                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Redundant transfer per 200 kB job | ~3 MB, Postgres → Edge Function |
| Steps that need the source text   | 2 of 12 (`chunk`, `synthesize`) |
| Steps that pay for it             | 10 of 12                        |

This is invisible today because the corpus is seeded and the hosted project has run one
verified job. It is not a micro-optimisation: `job_step_outputs` is `stable` and
`security definer`, PostgREST materialises the aggregate on every call, and the cost grows
with the number of steps — which section 2 proposes to increase.

It is also the clearest argument for the graph. A node that declares its inputs can be
served only those inputs; a step in a straight line cannot, because "what came before" is
the only addressing scheme a line has.

### 1.2 A structural `critic` failure destroys a paid-for synthesis

`critic` (`pipeline.ts:828`) throws when a Pull has an empty body or a headline over 200
characters. The worker records a failed attempt and leaves the message; the next tick
re-runs **`critic`**, reading the same stored `synthesize` output. It is deterministic, so
it fails identically three times and the job is marked `failed`.

The synthesis has been paid for and is discarded. There is no way to say _"this draft is
close, ask again with the defect named"_ because a straight line has no back-edge — the
only transition `nextStep` can express is forward by one.

This is a law 2 failure in the direction the law cares about: money spent, nothing served.
It has not been observed in production because `critic` is structural and the provider's
schema already enforces most of what it checks — which is another way of saying the gate
currently earns very little and cannot be made to earn more without a back-edge.

### 1.3 The taxonomy exists in four places and is checked in one

`TOPIC_SLUGS` (`providers.ts`), `SUMMARY_SCHEMA`'s enum (`gemini.ts:157`), Anthropic's
tool schema, and the seeded `public.topics` rows. `narrowTopics` (`pipeline.ts`) filters
against the first; nothing compares the others.

Partially addressed: `packages/prompts/src/topics.test.ts` now binds the BAML enum to
`TOPIC_SLUGS` in both directions. That closes one edge of the square. The two provider
schemas and the seeded rows are still unchecked, and section 3 is how the remaining edges
close — by there being only one of them.

### 1.4 `trustFromProvenance` reads the raw target URL, and its comment says it does not

`pipeline.ts:781` sets `sourceUrl` from `acquired.url` with the comment:

> `acquired.url` is the one `resolve_identity` validated, not the raw target — the same
> distinction the scores below make about re-deriving from input.

`pipeline.ts:792`, eleven lines below, passes `asString(job.target.url)`.

Latent rather than live: `resolve_identity` currently returns `asString(target.url)`
unchanged, so the two are byte-identical today. It becomes real the moment that step
normalises, canonicalises or resolves a redirect — which is exactly what closing the
remaining SSRF surface would involve. `trust_score` is part of the 0.24 of `get_feed`'s
score that `20260830200114` went to some length to protect from a hostile requester, so
the value being derived from the unvalidated input is the wrong side to be latent on.

Fix is one line. It is listed here rather than filed as a nit because the comment asserts
the opposite of the code, and a future reader is more likely to trust the comment.

### 1.5 Three steps exist to do nothing

`artwork` (`pipeline.ts:881`) returns a constant. `moderate` on a private job returns
immediately. `extract_evidence` computes section offsets that nothing downstream reads.

Each is a full Edge Function invocation, a `pg_net` dispatch and a `pg_cron` tick. The
roadmap already established the principle — _"the invocation is what costs, not the
tick"_ — when it added `jumpTo` so a reused job would not walk nine no-ops. The same
argument applies to a job that is _not_ reused: it walks three.

`artwork` being off is correct and `generation.md` explains why. Its being off _as a queue
hop_ is not.

### 1.6 Nothing writes `pull_relations`, and the Delta is waiting on it

Recorded in the roadmap, repeated here because it is the largest capability currently
dark. The negation-aware Delta — several migrations, a dedicated `db:test` file, and the
most carefully reasoned piece of work in the repository — is **live for the seed corpus
and inert for everything the pipeline generates**, because no step emits an `opposes`
edge.

Every generated contradiction is therefore filed as already-known and hidden, which is the
precise failure the negation work exists to prevent. This is a feature gap, not a bug, but
it is the one whose absence most changes what the product does.

### 1.7 Smaller notes

- **The front end concentrates.** `App.tsx` is 1,203 lines and holds routing, session,
  destination and tab state; `Feed.tsx` is 953 and `Auth.tsx` 830. Having no router and no
  data library is a deliberate and defensible choice, but the composition burden it
  removes from `package.json` lands in one file. Worth splitting session and routing out
  of `App.tsx` before round 3 adds Feed Builder to it.
- **`qualityFromDraft` never penalises padding.** Its comment says _"more is usually the
  model padding"_; `Math.min(1, pulls.length / 8)` saturates at 8 and treats 40 Pulls as
  ideal. A band rather than a floor would match the stated intent.
- **The source text is readable by the requester for seven days.**
  `job_steps_own` grants SELECT on `job_steps` to the job's requester, and
  `prune_operational_logs` nulls `output` seven days after the job finishes. So a
  signed-in reader can enqueue a URL and read back the verbatim text. Not a rights breach
  today — `DEFAULT_SOURCE_HOSTS` restricts fetching to three public-domain archives — but
  the entire law 4 posture on this path rests on that allowlist alone, and the roadmap
  plans to widen it for the Pull Studio. The text does not need to be in `output` at all
  once nodes declare inputs (§1.1); a content-addressed scratch row that is never
  reader-readable is the shape that survives the Studio.

## 2. Graph engineering

### 2.1 What is actually being claimed

The article's claim, stripped of the naming event around it: a loop is one agent acting
toward a goal with a verifier and a stop condition, and a graph is what you build when one
loop is no longer enough. A graph still contains loops — a node can have an edge back to
an earlier node — but the loop becomes _one edge among many on a map you can look at_,
rather than being the whole architecture.

The sceptical reading is also right and worth keeping: graph engineering is loop
engineering with an org chart drawn on top, and rebuilding a working system to acquire a
diagram is how you spend a quarter for nothing.

So the question is not whether graphs are fashionable. It is whether this pipeline is
paying a price a line charges and a graph does not. It is, in four specific places:

| The line cannot express | What it costs today                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| A node's inputs         | Every step reads every earlier output (§1.1)            |
| A back-edge             | A critic failure discards a paid synthesis (§1.2)       |
| Two nodes at once       | `embed`, `artwork`, `relations` serialise for no reason |
| A join                  | Nowhere to add a node that needs two predecessors       |

`STEPS` is a `readonly string[]` and `nextStep` is `STEPS[indexOf(current) + 1]`. There is
already one exception carved into it — `jumpTo`, added so a reused job could skip nine
steps — and the honest description of that is _the first edge in a graph, expressed as a
special case because there was nowhere to declare it._

### 2.2 The proposed graph

```
resolve_identity
      │
   acquire ──────────── reuse? ─────────────────────────┐
      │                                                 │
    chunk                                               │
      ├──────────────┐                                  │
extract_evidence  synthesize ◄────── revise ────┐       │
      │               │                         │       │
      └──────┬────────┘                         │       │
             ▼                                  │       │
          critic ───── verdict: revise ─────────┘       │
             │            (bounded, max 2)              │
        verdict: pass                                   │
             ▼                                          │
         template                                       │
             ▼                                          │
          cards                                         │
             ├──────────┬──────────┐                    │
             ▼          ▼          ▼                    │
          embed    relations    artwork                 │
             └──────────┴──────────┘                    │
                        ▼                               │
                    moderate ◄──────────────────────────┘
                        ▼
                     publish
```

Four things this expresses that the line cannot:

1. **`extract_evidence` and `synthesize` both depend only on `chunk`** — so they run
   concurrently, and `critic` is the join that needs both. Today `extract_evidence` is a
   step that produces spans nothing reads; in the graph it is the evidence a real critic
   is checked against.
2. **`critic → synthesize` is a bounded back-edge.** A draft that fails is re-requested
   once with the defect named, rather than retried identically three times and discarded.
3. **`embed`, `relations` and `artwork` fan out** from `cards` and join at `moderate`.
   Three independent provider calls, independently retryable, no ordering between them
   because none exists.
4. **Reuse is an ordinary conditional edge**, not a `jumpTo` field on a result type.

### 2.3 How it is executed

The constraint that shaped the current design has not gone away: **Edge Functions cap at
150 s wall clock, so one invocation runs one node.** The graph does not change that. It
changes what an invocation is told and where it may go next.

Three pieces of work, all additive:

**(a) Nodes declare their inputs.** `_shared/graph.ts` replaces `steps.ts`:

```ts
export const NODES = {
  synthesize: { needs: ['acquire'],            after: ['chunk'] },
  critic:     { needs: ['synthesize', 'extract_evidence'], after: [...] },
  moderate:   { needs: [],                     after: ['embed', 'relations', 'artwork'] },
  // …
} as const;
```

`needs` is what the node reads; `after` is what must have succeeded first. They are
different, and separating them is most of the value: `moderate` waits for three nodes and
reads none of their output.

**(b) `job_step_outputs` takes a step list.** A new migration adds
`job_step_outputs(p_job_id uuid, p_steps text[])`, and the worker passes
`NODES[step].needs`. The zero-argument form stays for replay compatibility. §1.1 closes
here, and it closes _more_ the more nodes there are — which is what makes adding `critic`
and `relations` affordable.

**(c) Advancing becomes a fan-out with a join.** This is the genuinely hard part and
should not be understated.

`advance_generation_job(p_job_id, p_from_step, p_to_step)` sends one message under a
compare-and-set on `current_step`. With parallel nodes, `current_step` stops being a
scalar and the compare-and-set stops being the thing that prevents a double dispatch.

The replacement:

- A node completing asks, for each successor, _are all of that successor's `after` nodes
  succeeded?_ — a SQL function over `job_steps`, so it reads committed state rather than
  the worker's belief.
- Dispatch is guarded by `insert into generation_dispatches (job_id, step) on conflict do
nothing`, and only a row that was actually inserted sends a message. Two parallel nodes
  finishing at the same instant therefore produce exactly one `moderate` message, and the
  guarantee comes from a unique index rather than from timing.
- `generation_jobs.current_step` is kept and becomes advisory — the frontier is derived
  from `job_steps` and `generation_dispatches`. Existing rows keep working; nothing needs
  backfilling.

Everything above is one append-only migration and a rewrite of `advance` in the worker.
`runPipelineStep` itself changes very little: it is already a `switch` over a step name
with no knowledge of what follows.

### 2.4 What could go wrong, stated up front

- **A join node that never fires.** If one of three parallel nodes fails permanently,
  `moderate` waits forever and the job sits in `running` with no queued message. The
  current design cannot produce this failure, and it is strictly worse than the one it
  replaces. The mitigation is a sweeper: a `pg_cron` tick that fails any job whose newest
  `job_steps` row is older than the visibility timeout and which has nothing queued. That
  sweeper does not exist today and **must land in the same PR as the fan-out**, not after
  it.
- **A back-edge that does not terminate.** `critic → synthesize` needs a revision counter
  persisted on the job, not held by the worker, and a hard cap of 2. An unbounded critic
  loop is a provider bill with no ceiling, which is the worst failure available in this
  system.
- **Replay of in-flight jobs.** Jobs queued under the linear machine must still complete.
  `NODES` is a superset of `STEPS` with the same names, `nextStep`'s behaviour is
  preserved for any node with a single successor, and the new `job_step_outputs` overload
  is additive — so an in-flight job simply walks a path that happens to be a line.

### 2.5 What is deliberately not adopted

No graph framework. LangGraph and its relatives assume a long-lived process that holds
state between nodes; this pipeline's defining constraint is that it _cannot_, and its
state already lives durably in `job_steps` and `pgmq`. Adding a framework would mean
running its state machine on top of the one Postgres is already running, and the two would
disagree the first time a worker died.

The graph here is ~60 lines of TypeScript declaration and one migration. That is the whole
of it, and it should stay that way.

## 3. Where BAML fits

`packages/prompts` is set up and `WriteCanonicalSummary` mirrors the current synthesis
contract. `docs/baml.md` states the constraint that decides everything else: **BAML's
TypeScript runtime is a native Node addon and Edge Functions are Deno isolates**, so
`baml_client` cannot be imported by the worker.

**Decided.** The `baml-cli serve` sidecar is the near-term crossing, and getting BAML into
the Edge Function properly is its own non-blocking track.

### 3.1 Near-term: the sidecar

BAML runs as an HTTP service exposing each function; the worker posts to it. Three things
it must get right, and the third is the one that can quietly break law 2:

1. **It is not publicly reachable.** An open BAML endpoint is an open proxy to paid model
   calls. Authenticate with a shared token from Vault, reusing the pattern
   `worker_dispatch_token` and `authorised()` already establish — including failing closed,
   so "misconfigured" cannot mean "public".

2. **Law 7 gains a row.** The credential table names Edge Function secrets or Vault; a
   sidecar holding `GOOGLE_AI_API_KEY` is neither. Amending a law is its own PR with its own
   reasoning, not a line added while doing something else — and it lands _before_ the
   sidecar, because putting a provider key somewhere new before deciding it belongs there is
   the wrong order.

3. **Do not route through BAML's `fallback` client.** `createFallbackSummaryProvider` falls
   through only on `ProviderUnavailableError` and rethrows `BilledProviderError`, precisely
   so a _billed_ failure reaches `cost_ledger` rather than being retried elsewhere for
   free-looking. BAML's `fallback` provider draws no such distinction and would swallow the
   charge. So **each BAML function names a single client, and retry and fallback
   orchestration stay in the worker where the ledger is.** `SummaryChain` in `clients.baml`
   is documentation of the intended order rather than something to call — its own comment
   already says so.

Deployment target is a real choice, to be made in the sidecar PR rather than assumed. A
container running `baml-cli serve` adds a third platform; a Vercel Node function importing
`baml_client` directly runs where `apps/web` already deploys and loads the addon natively.
The second is probably right. Both put a provider key somewhere new, which is why (2)
exists.

### 3.2 Track: get BAML into the Edge Function

Not blocking, and worth real investigation, because succeeding removes the hop, the sidecar
and the law amendment together.

The constraint restated, since it is what the track has to defeat: BAML's TypeScript runtime
is a **native napi addon** and Supabase Edge Functions are **Deno isolates on a restricted
runtime with no FFI and no native addon loader**. No `output_type` changes that — every
generator emits TypeScript that imports the addon.

The concrete lead: BAML's VSCode playground renders prompts in a browser, so a WASM build
exists. `@gloo-ai/baml-schema-wasm-web` is on npm at 0.89.1. Two things to establish before
believing anything: it is a _different and much older package line_ than the 0.226.2 runtime,
and it is the **schema** build — so the open question is whether it can render a prompt and
emit an output schema for a named function, not whether it can make the call. It does not
need to make the call; the existing Deno providers already do that well.

If it can, a build-time export into `supabase/functions/_shared/generated/` closes §1.3
completely with no runtime dependency crossing the boundary at all. **Timebox to one day and
accept a negative result** — the sidecar works, so this is an improvement rather than a
prerequisite.

### 3.3 The BAML functions Fable 5.1 adds

```
WriteCanonicalSummary   — exists, mirrors today's synthesis
CritiqueSummary         — new. (summary, evidence spans) → verdict | issues | revision hint
ExtractRelations        — new. (pulls) → edges: supports | opposes | extends | exemplifies
```

`CritiqueSummary` is what makes the back-edge worth having. `ExtractRelations` is what
turns the negation-aware Delta on for generated content (§1.6).

**Cost, because law 2 requires the number and not the intention.** Both run over the
_draft_, not the source, so their input is a few thousand tokens rather than fifty
thousand:

|                                        | input | output |     approx. |
| -------------------------------------- | ----: | -----: | ----------: |
| synthesize (today)                     |   50k |     4k |     $0.0148 |
| CritiqueSummary                        |   ~5k |  ~0.5k |    ~$0.0020 |
| ExtractRelations                       |   ~5k |    ~1k |    ~$0.0025 |
| revision (fires on a minority of jobs) |   50k |     4k | $0.0148 × p |

At a 15% revision rate that is **~$0.0215 per canonical summary against $0.0148 today** —
a 45% increase on text generation, and still under half the cost of the one illustration
`generation.md` already calls the first thing to switch off. Both new calls write to
`cost_ledger` through the existing `PROVIDER_STEPS` mechanism, which needs two entries
added and nothing else.

The ratio law 2 actually defends is unchanged: this is still once per canonical summary,
amortised across every reader.

## 4. Features

Ordered by value per unit of risk, not by appeal.

### 4.1 Wire up `refresh_knowledge_vector` — highest value, no new cost

`user_knowledge_vectors` is never populated, so the `uvec` term — **18% of `get_feed`'s
score and the largest personalisation signal in it** — evaluates to the same constant for
every card and every reader. The feed is currently ranked by topic weights, quality,
novelty and jitter alone.

The function exists and is tested. What is missing is a caller. A `pg_cron` tick
recomputing centroids for readers with new history since the last run is a migration and
nothing else, and the roadmap already identified this as the round 2 decision. It is the
single largest improvement to what a reader sees that this plan contains, and it involves
no model, no new table and no new cost.

Alternative, if measurement says the centroid does not earn its write amplification: drop
the term and redistribute its 18%. Either is better than a constant. **Decide with a
measurement, not in this document.**

### 4.2 Relation extraction — turns on the Delta's best work

The `ExtractRelations` node from §3.3, plus the backfill of the existing generated corpus.
Unblocks the negation-aware Delta for everything the pipeline produces, and is the
prerequisite the roadmap names for closing the Delta's stated incompleteness (a reader who
knows an unannotated restatement still has the contradiction hidden — edges dense enough
to describe claims rather than pulls are what fix it).

### 4.3 Claim anchors

`citation_anchors` is written by nothing. `extract_evidence` currently produces section
offsets that no step reads. With the graph, `critic` reads them — and once a claim can be
checked against a span, writing the anchor is the same work. Law 4's "analysis, not
reproduction" argument is materially stronger when every claim points at where it came
from.

### 4.4 Close the duplicate-bill race

The roadmap's open risk: two jobs fingerprinting the same source can both pay. The
proposed `generation_hash_claims` row with a lease was deferred because a crashed job
holding a claim blocks every later request for that source — trading a duplicated bill for
a stalled queue.

The sweeper §2.4 requires for the fan-out is the same sweeper this needs. Building them
together is what makes the lease safe, and is the reason this lands in 5.1 rather than
staying deferred.

### 4.5 Onboarding

The roadmap's oldest carried gap: new readers get default preferences and an unweighted
feed. With §4.1 done, a reader's first session can seed a knowledge vector from a handful
of stated interests rather than waiting for history to accumulate. Sequenced after §4.1
for that reason.

### 4.6 Smaller, and worth doing while adjacent

- The Delta banner counting the pool rather than the page (roadmap; a counting-scope
  decision, not a maths bug).
- Classifying permanently-invalid offline writes so a 404 stops retrying forever while a
  500 still does.
- §1.4's one-line fix, and §1.7's `qualityFromDraft` band.

## 6. Definition of done

Unchanged from `CLAUDE.md`, with three additions specific to this work:

- A generation job that fans out and rejoins is verified **end to end on the hosted
  project**, not only against fakes — the roadmap records that the first version of the
  twelve steps looked finished and could not have completed a single job, because four
  values TypeScript accepted were refused by Postgres.
- The sweeper is demonstrated against a deliberately stranded job, not reasoned about.
- Every new provider call has a `cost_ledger` row in that verified run, and the run's
  total is compared against the estimate in §3.3. A cost model nobody checked against a
  bill is a guess with a table around it.
